## 1. Golden rule

A Reel is not a complete explanation. It is a controlled sequence of unanswered questions.

The viewer should understand the current line while still wanting one missing piece from the next. Curiosity without clarity feels confusing. Clarity without an unanswered question feels finished.

Use this playbook for Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, talking-head videos, voiceovers, serialized short-form content, hooks, and retention audits.

## 2. Core workflow

Classify the request as **create**, **rewrite**, **audit**, or **series design**.

Before writing, identify:

- one viewer;
- one big idea;
- one opening conflict or tension;
- one question the hook creates;
- one reason the outcome matters;
- one visible or emotionally specific moment;
- one promised payoff;
- one final action or next-episode loop.

If the script needs several unrelated ideas, split it into a series.

Build the retention spine:

1. **Pattern interrupt:** enter at the first interesting moment, usually conflict rather than context.
2. **Curiosity:** create a concrete question without hiding the subject behind vague clickbait.
3. **Escalation:** use causality, reversal, and consequence to make each line require the next.
4. **Stakes:** show what can be gained, lost, exposed, or changed.
5. **Specific moment:** replace abstract explanation with something viewers can picture or hear.
6. **Payoff:** resolve the main promise before asking for anything.
7. **Exit:** use one relevant CTA, conclusion, or open loop for the next episode.

Track open loops while drafting:

| Loop | Opened by | Resolved by | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main question | Hook | Core payoff | Must close |
| Emotional question | Twist or confession | Stakes or realization | Usually close |
| Series question | Final line | Next Reel | May remain open |

Do not open more loops than the viewer can remember. Never replace the promised payoff with a follow request.

## 3. Twelve rules

### Rule 1: Start with conflict, not context

Weak:

> Hey, I'm Mariia, and I've been a designer for two years.

Stronger:

> Today, I'm stealing a developer's job.

Enter at the first meaningful change, contradiction, decision, failure, or risk. Introduce the creator after attention has been earned.

### Rule 2: Use one big idea

Do not combine a career story, several tools, an industry debate, procrastination, portfolio advice, and an entire build process in one Reel.

Reduce the episode to one sentence:

> Can a graphic designer build her own website with AI?

If a second idea is still valuable, make it the next episode.

### Rule 3: Make the hook create a question

The first line should make the viewer wonder something concrete:

- What is being built?
- Can this person actually do it?
- Why are they refusing the obvious option?
- What went wrong?
- What happens if they fail?

Do not answer the premise, method, result, and lesson in the opening line.

### Rule 4: Open and close loops deliberately

Example:

> Today, I'm stealing a developer's job.\
> And I have absolutely no idea what I'm doing.\
> But that isn't the embarrassing part.\
> I've been a graphic designer for two years, and I still do not have a portfolio.

Open a new loop as another closes. Keep the main promise easy to remember.

### Rule 5: Use `but` and `so` for momentum

`And then` often reports chronology. `But` introduces resistance. `So` introduces consequence or decision.

> I needed a portfolio, **but** developers were outside my budget.\
> **So** I decided to build it with AI.\
> **But** three months later, it was still unfinished.\
> **So** I decided to build it publicly.

Every beat changes the situation.

### Rule 6: Put stakes near the beginning

Explain why the experiment matters before demonstrating the process:

> Without a portfolio, potential clients cannot see what I can do.

Or:

> I am starting to wonder how many opportunities this unfinished website has already cost me.

Stakes may be practical, emotional, social, financial, reputational, or identity-based. Keep them proportional and true.

### Rule 7: Replace abstractions with visible moments

Weak:

> Creating a portfolio is overwhelming.

Visible:

> I opened a blank Webflow project, stared at it for 20 minutes, and closed it.

Weak:

> It is still very unfinished.

Visible:

> Three months later, the homepage still ends after the second section.

Let viewers see behavior, objects, screens, time, numbers, reactions, and consequences.

### Rule 8: Write for the ear

Use short, speakable lines:

> I can design.\
> I cannot code.\
> So naturally, I decided to build a website.

Read the script aloud at performance energy. Shorten any line that forces an unnatural breath or loses emphasis before its final word.

### Rule 9: Turn `I` into `you`

A personal story earns attention. Shared recognition creates connection.

> And if you have also postponed your portfolio because it feels overwhelming, I am figuring it out for both of us.

Do not force a generic lesson. Connect through the exact situation, emotion, or obstacle the viewer shares.

### Rule 10: Promise a specific payoff

Weak:

> Follow my journey.

Specific:

> I am sharing every prompt, mistake, and decision until this website is live.

State what future attention buys: templates, decisions, mistakes, results, transformations, entertainment, or access.

### Rule 11: Use true tension, not fake controversy

Weak:

> I hate developers.

Truthful tension:

> I cannot afford the website I want, so I am trying to build it myself with AI.

Conflict can come from constraints, contradiction, vulnerability, ambition, risk, or a difficult choice. Manufactured anger may win a click while destroying trust.

### Rule 12: Cut anything without a narrative job

Remove:

- repeated explanations;
- long introductions;
- generic motivation;
- defensive disclaimers;
- context required only later;
- setup that the visual already communicates;
- details that belong in another episode.

Compression is not a fixed percentage. Cut until every line creates curiosity, emotion, clarity, proof, or progress while preserving the creator's voice.

## 4. Repeatable structures

### Experiment Reel

1. Pattern interrupt
2. Capability gap or constraint
3. Stakes
4. Experiment
5. Risk
6. Result or current state
7. Specific series promise
8. Next obstacle

Example spine:

> Today, I'm stealing a developer's job.\
> I can design, but I cannot code.\
> And after two years, I still do not have the portfolio my work deserves.\
> So I am using AI to build the entire website myself.\
> It could become my best project or a very public disaster.\
> Either way, I am sharing every prompt, mistake, and decision until it is live.\
> And the first problem was not the design. It was deciding what the portfolio needed to prove.

### Mistake Reel

1. Unexpected failure
2. Expected outcome
3. Visible evidence of failure
4. Hidden cause
5. Correction
6. Result
7. Viewer takeaway

### Transformation Reel

1. Concrete before state
2. Failed or incomplete attempt
3. Insight or mechanism
4. Action
5. Concrete after state
6. Honest limitation
7. Replicable next step

### Opinion Reel

1. Specific claim
2. Familiar belief it contradicts
3. Cost of the familiar belief
4. Evidence or example
5. Nuanced boundary
6. Better rule

### Tutorial Reel

1. Outcome first
2. Why the common method fails
3. Minimum required steps
4. Visible demonstration
5. Common mistake
6. Result
7. Save or apply CTA

Keep tutorials narrow enough to complete the promised result. Move edge cases to captions, comments, or another episode.

## 5. Spoken and visual writing

### Spoken pass

- Mark natural breaths with line breaks.
- Put the most important word near the end of a line when it strengthens delivery.
- Prefer one image or action per sentence.
- Remove nested clauses and formal transitions.
- Keep deliberate fragments; remove accidental confusion.
- Preserve vocabulary the creator would actually say on camera.

### Visual pass

For every beat, ask:

- Can the viewer see the claim?
- Is the visual adding evidence or merely decorating the sentence?
- Can the visual replace spoken setup?
- Does the on-screen text remain readable before the shot changes?
- Is the first frame understandable without audio?

When production detail is requested, use:

| Beat | Voice | Visual | On-screen text | Narrative job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Otherwise, return a clean spoken script without forcing a production table.

## 6. Series and variant design

### Series design

Define:

- **Season promise:** what viewers receive by following the whole sequence.
- **End condition:** what makes the series complete.
- **Episode idea:** one problem, decision, experiment, or result.
- **Episode payoff:** what this Reel resolves on its own.
- **Bridge loop:** the next specific obstacle or decision.

Do not make every episode pure setup. Each Reel must deliver local value before opening the next loop.

### Variant design

Change one meaningful variable per variant:

| Variable | Example hypothesis |
| --- | --- |
| Conflict | Budget constraint creates more identification than lack of technical skill |
| Curiosity | An unexplained visible failure holds attention better than a broad question |
| Stakes | Opportunity cost matters more than embarrassment |
| Proof | Showing the unfinished page earns more trust than describing procrastination |
| Viewer connection | A direct mirror line increases saves and comments |
| CTA | A concrete series promise earns more follows than `follow my journey` |

Keep the facts, offer, and payoff stable. Treat performance claims as hypotheses until watch-time, retention, completion, saves, shares, comments, or conversions confirm them.

## 7. Final checklist

- Does the first sentence interrupt passive scrolling?
- Does it create a concrete unanswered question?
- Is there one clear central idea?
- Are the stakes visible early?
- Does every sentence cause or require the next?
- Can viewers picture what is being said?
- Is there truthful emotional tension, vulnerability, or consequence?
- Does the script deliver the payoff promised by the hook?
- Is the personal story connected to the viewer?
- Does the CTA promise specific value or action?
- If this is a series, does the episode close one loop before opening the next?
- Can another line be removed without weakening clarity, emotion, proof, or progress?

Remember:

> Hook with conflict. Continue with curiosity. Connect through emotion. Finish with a payoff or a new question.
