# Sugarman Writing Process

Use this process for copy created from scratch or substantially restructured. For a light correction, preserve the original structure and load only the relevant editing pass.

## 1. Become the product expert

Collect enough detail to explain the product naturally and answer a skeptical buyer:

- origin and reason it exists;
- exact features and how they work;
- differentiators and category expectations;
- use cases, limitations, service, delivery, guarantee, and price;
- customer habits, alternatives, prior attempts, and the cost of doing nothing;
- proof: demonstrations, examples, testimonials, numbers, screenshots, credentials, and technical detail.

Do not turn every fact into copy. Expertise gives you the freedom to find the one emotionally important point and the details that make it credible.

## 2. Find the product-prospect fit

Write one line for each:

- **Product nature:** the emotional character the category naturally carries, such as playful, serious, safe, technical, premium, immediate, or reassuring.
- **Prospect nature:** what the reader already wants, fears, believes, recognizes, and expects from this category.
- **Existing desire:** the demand already present in the market. Copy channels desire; it does not manufacture it from nothing.
- **Required belief:** what must become true in the reader's mind before the offer feels like the obvious next step.

The tone, mechanism, proof, and promise must harmonize with both natures. A mismatch creates resistance even when every sentence is polished.

## 3. Develop the concept

Start with `I want to sell this product`, then move through:

1. What unique or newly relevant aspect should the prospect notice?
2. What familiar idea can make that aspect instantly understandable?
3. What old belief keeps the prospect stuck?
4. What new belief makes the product a natural bridge?
5. What single promise expresses the resulting transformation credibly?

Generate several concepts, then keep the one that best combines desire, differentiation, simplicity, and proof. Let the material rest when the schedule allows; otherwise perform a separate fresh-context concept review before drafting.

Useful concept shapes:

- `old way -> hidden cost -> new way -> product`;
- `familiar object or idea -> surprising connection -> product becomes easy to grasp`;
- `tolerated problem -> concrete consequence -> distinct mechanism -> believable result`.

## 4. Map the question chain

Before prose, outline the silent conversation in the reader's head. A typical chain is:

1. Why should I pay attention?
2. Is this about someone like me?
3. Why is my current approach failing?
4. What is different here?
5. How does it work?
6. Can I believe this?
7. What will it change for me?
8. Why this option instead of the alternatives?
9. What exactly do I get and what does it cost?
10. What could still go wrong?
11. Why should I act now?
12. What do I do next?

Adapt the order to the reader's awareness and the offer. Put the answer immediately after the question is likely to arise. Each answer should resolve tension while creating a relevant reason to read the next block.

## 5. Draft emotionally

- Write the first draft for meaning, feeling, images, and momentum. Expect it to be rough.
- Keep the headline and first sentence short enough to make entering the copy effortless.
- Use words whose associations support the desired emotion and brand character.
- Use stories when they embody the concept, build relationship, or demonstrate the mechanism.
- Let the reader mentally use the product and experience the result before asking for action.
- Follow emotional desire with the logical facts needed to justify the decision.

Curiosity is useful only when the withheld answer matters. Close every open loop you create.

## 6. Edit analytically

Run distinct passes instead of trying to solve everything sentence by sentence:

1. **Continuity:** does every element make the next one easier to consume?
2. **Questions:** does the sequence answer doubts at the moment they appear?
3. **Belief:** does each block move the reader from the current belief toward the required one?
4. **Credibility:** is every claim supported by mechanism, specificity, proof, or an honest limitation?
5. **Compression:** remove throat-clearing, duplicated ideas, filler transitions, unnecessary clauses, and claims weaker than the strongest argument.
6. **Rhythm:** vary sentence length, paragraph length, and emphasis without creating a mechanical pattern.
7. **Voice:** restore the author's vocabulary, directness, humor, and rough edges that carry personality.
8. **Offer:** summarize what the buyer gets, the price and terms, risk reversal, real urgency, and one direct CTA.
9. **Proofread:** verify spelling, links, placeholders, numbers, dates, names, formatting, and factual consistency.

Compression is successful when the same emotion and argument arrive faster. A percentage reduction is not the goal.

## 7. Final read-through

Read the copy as the prospect, not as its author. Stop at each point where attention, comprehension, belief, or confidence drops. Repair the cause, then check the transition into the next block.

The copy is complete when:

- the entry feels effortless;
- one concept organizes the whole argument;
- the reader never has to hold an unanswered blocking objection;
- emotion creates desire and logic makes acting defensible;
- all promises are supported and all open loops close;
- the CTA is the natural next step, not an abrupt request.
